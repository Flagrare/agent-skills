## Question

What do recognised practitioners and researchers say actually gets an engineer promoted (especially to senior and staff), what are the mechanics beyond doing good work, and what goes wrong?

## Method note

Three research agents read sources through a summarising fetch tool. Every quote below marked "(verified)" was then matched against the raw page text. Claims marked "(snippet)" came from search results only and were not checked against the source.

## Sources

### [Getting the title where you are](https://staffeng.com/guides/getting-the-title-where-you-are/), [Promotion packets](https://staffeng.com/guides/promo-packets/), [Find your sponsor](https://staffeng.com/guides/find-your-sponsor/)
- **Authors / Org:** Will Larson, StaffEng
- **Type:** practitioner guide
- **Published:** unknown
- **Accessed:** 2026-09-30
- **Relevance:** high
- **What this contributed:** The clearest statement that the next level is a different job, not a better version of the current one. It also says a sponsor (almost always the direct manager) and leadership awareness are required, and that the packet is a planning tool you start early.
- **Quoted (verified):**
  > "A Staff engineer isn't a better Senior engineer, but someone who's moved into fulfilling one of the Staff archetypes."
  > "You can only change a system with sponsorship from within."
  > "you won't get promoted if your company's leadership doesn't know who you are."
  > "this almost always needs to be your direct manager" (the sponsor)
  > "your packet becomes the map to accomplishing your goal." / "Promotions, especially at this level, are built over quarters, halves, and years."

### [Software Developer Promotions: Advice to Get to That Next Level](https://blog.pragmaticengineer.com/software-engineering-promotions/)
- **Authors / Org:** Gergely Orosz, The Pragmatic Engineer
- **Type:** engineering blog
- **Published:** 2019-10-01 (updated 2021)
- **Accessed:** 2026-09-30
- **Relevance:** high
- **What this contributed:** The "already performing there" rule, the manager's incentive to promote you, and the non-merit limits (budget above senior).
- **Quoted (verified):**
  > "Most tech companies follow an approach of promoting people to the next level, who are already performing there."
  > "It is in the best interest of your manager for you to be promoted - when you are ready."
  > "there might be a budgeting limit to how many people can be promoted to higher levels."

### [How I got promoted to staff engineer twice](https://www.seangoedecke.com/staff-engineer-promotions/)
- **Authors / Org:** Sean Goedecke
- **Type:** engineering blog
- **Published:** 2024-12-28
- **Accessed:** 2026-09-30
- **Relevance:** high
- **What this contributed:** The sharpest dissent from the "be technically excellent" view: promotions follow value on what the company currently cares about, and need a manager who is fully on board.
- **Quoted (verified):**
  > "Promotions do not happen because a particular engineer is really technically strong and ready for the next level."
  > "You are very unlikely to be promoted to staff engineer for working on issues the company does not currently care about"
  > "you do not get to staff by being a great mentor or leader. You get to staff by delivering value for the company."
  > "your manager has to be switched on and fully on board."

### [Being Glue](https://noidea.dog/glue)
- **Authors / Org:** Tanya Reilly
- **Type:** conference talk / essay
- **Published:** unknown (circa 2019)
- **Accessed:** 2026-09-30
- **Relevance:** high
- **What this contributed:** The main caution. Coordination and unblocking work helps the team but doesn't build a promotion case on its own.
- **Quoted (verified):**
  > "If you only do glue, you will only get better at glue. You're making your team more effective but you're hurting your future self."
  > "if someone's doing very little of their core job, they are hurting their career."

### [Get your work recognized: write a brag document](https://jvns.ca/blog/brag-documents/)
- **Authors / Org:** Julia Evans
- **Type:** engineering blog
- **Published:** 2019-06-28
- **Accessed:** 2026-09-30
- **Relevance:** medium
- **What this contributed:** Good work is not automatically seen, and your manager needs written material to advocate for you.
- **Quoted (verified):**
  > "if you do great work at your job, people will (or should!) automatically recognize that work and reward you for it with promotions / increased pay. In practice, it's often more complicated than that"
  > "if your manager is going to effectively advocate for you, they need help."

### [What does sponsorship look like?](https://larahogan.me/blog/what-sponsorship-looks-like/)
- **Authors / Org:** Lara Hogan
- **Type:** engineering-management blog
- **Published:** 2017-05-14
- **Accessed:** 2026-09-30
- **Relevance:** medium
- **What this contributed:** The mentor/sponsor distinction and concrete sponsor actions (nominating people to lead projects, forwarding their work to wider audiences). It cites the Ibarra HBR research.
- **Quoted (verified):** "over-mentored, but under-sponsored"

### [Why Men Still Get More Promotions Than Women](https://hbr.org/2010/09/why-men-still-get-more-promotions-than-women)
- **Authors / Org:** Herminia Ibarra, Nancy M. Carter, Christine Silva, Harvard Business Review
- **Type:** research article (secondary summary of a Catalyst survey)
- **Published:** 2010-09
- **Accessed:** 2026-09-30 (paywalled, only the opening was readable)
- **Relevance:** medium
- **What this contributed:** The origin of the sponsor-versus-mentor finding: sponsors advocate and create visibility, mentors advise. The survey figures (4,000+ high-potential employees, a pay gap in first post-MBA jobs) are snippet-only and not verified.

### [Gender Differences in Accepting and Receiving Requests for Tasks with Low Promotability](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257%2Faer.20141734)
- **Authors / Org:** Linda Babcock, Maria P. Recalde, Lise Vesterlund, Laurie Weingart, American Economic Review 107(3)
- **Type:** academic paper
- **Published:** 2017-03
- **Accessed:** 2026-09-30
- **Relevance:** medium
- **What this contributed:** The empirical base under "glue work". Low-promotability tasks are real, and who ends up doing them is shaped by expectations, not only by choice.
- **Quoted (verified, abstract):** women, more than men, "volunteer, are asked to volunteer, and accept requests to volunteer for such tasks. Beliefs that women, more than men, say yes to tasks with low promotability appear as an important driver of these differences."
- The lab figures (women 48% more likely to volunteer in mixed groups, 44% more likely to be asked) are snippet-only.

### [Promotions and the Peter Principle](https://www.nber.org/papers/w24343)
- **Authors / Org:** Alan Benson, Danielle Li, Kelly Shue (NBER working paper, later QJE 2019)
- **Type:** academic paper
- **Published:** 2018-02 (NBER)
- **Accessed:** 2026-09-30
- **Relevance:** medium
- **What this contributed:** Evidence that firms over-weight current-job performance when promoting, which is why ladders and committees ask for evidence of next-level behavior rather than excellence at the current level. The data is from sales workers, not engineers.
- **Quoted (verified, abstract):** "using microdata on the performance of sales workers at 214 firms, we find evidence consistent with the 'Peter principle,' which predicts that firms prioritize current job performance in promotion decisions at the expense of other observable characteristics that better predict managerial performance."

These are anecdotal.
- Dropbox's public career framework ([IC3](https://dropbox.github.io/dbx-career-framework/ic3_software_engineer.html), [IC4](https://dropbox.github.io/dbx-career-framework/ic4_software_engineer.html)): the step up is from owning "projects in service of quarterly goals" to owning "semi-annual/annual goals" and "technical roadmaps of larger projects, often involving cross-team dependencies" (fetch summary).

## Synthesis

**The experts converge on two conditions, not one.**

1. **You're already doing the next job, and it's a different job.** Orosz: companies promote people "who are already performing there". Larson: the next level "isn't a better Senior engineer". Dropbox's ladder shows the step concretely: a longer horizon, owning the roadmap, cross-team work. The Peter Principle study explains why committees insist on this: firms that promote on current-job excellence pick worse managers.
2. **Someone with power sees it and argues for it.** Larson: "you can only change a system with sponsorship from within", and the sponsor is "almost always" your direct manager. Goedecke: your manager "has to be switched on and fully on board". Evans: your manager "need[s] help", so write it down. Hogan and Ibarra: mentors advise, sponsors spend their credibility on you.

**Two refinements matter most for a mid-level engineer:**
- **Impact counts only on what the company cares about now.** Goedecke is blunt: technical strength and mentoring alone don't do it, and work on issues the company "does not currently care about" won't be promoted, even if it's important.
- **Glue work helps the team but doesn't build a case on its own.** Reilly: "If you only do glue, you will only get better at glue." Babcock et al. show this work is unevenly handed out, so it's worth checking whether you're being asked more than peers.

**What's outside your control:** budget above senior (Orosz), manager quality and fit (Goedecke), timing and committee calibration. Advice that ignores these oversells how much individual effort decides.

**Dissent:** Majors reminds readers that climbing a ladder is a career choice, not an automatic upgrade. The promotion-driven-development critique warns that packet incentives can reward launches over maintenance. No rigorous study was found showing that visibility advice rewards politics over impact. The sponsorship research is survey-based and correlational.
